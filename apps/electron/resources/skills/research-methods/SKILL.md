# Research methods

Translate the assigned question into observable quantities, assumptions and a comparison that could refute the hypothesis. Choose a method appropriate to the data and decision: descriptive analysis, statistical estimation, simulation, a controlled experiment or a predictive evaluation. Explain why the available evidence supports that choice.

Inspect provenance, dates, permissions, sampling, missing values, duplicates, units and definitions. Preserve the raw inputs and record transformations. Align business, financial, scientific or operational data to the same time basis and scope before comparing it. In prediction, keep training and evaluation inputs separate and avoid future leakage.

Establish a useful baseline and record the code, dependency versions, parameters, random seeds where relevant, commands and result paths. Run the assigned analysis with available tools and recompute key totals or metrics. Treat planned experiments, literature claims and executed measurements as different evidence levels.

Report effect sizes or errors and relevant uncertainty, negative results, sensitivity to assumptions and coverage limits. Correlation alone does not prove causation; model scores are not calibrated truth probabilities without suitable validation. Connect conclusions to the actual question, data and decision conditions instead of extrapolating to unsupported populations or dates.

Deliver reusable results and methods for independent checking. If data or tools are missing, provide the supported partial result and a concrete next verification step. In a Super Agent team, use assigned paths and sources and return evidence to the orchestrator without changing the goal or authorizing external business actions.
